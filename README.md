# Linking predicted and measured microbial carbon use efficiency in soils from contrasting precipitation regimes
**Abstract for the study**
>Microbial carbon use efficiency (CUE), the proportion of assimilated carbon (C) allocated to growth rather than respiration, influences whether soil organic C persists or is lost as carbon dioxide. However, it remains unclear whether long-term precipitation regimes and nutrient inputs alter microbial C allocation, and whether genome-based metabolic models capture these responses. We investigated these relationships in soils from two New Zealand farms with contrasting median annual precipitation (MAP; 550 and 2,779 mm) during a 72-day incubation with <sup>13</sup>C-labelled roots, synthetic urine, or both. We quantified root-derived C allocation using <sup>13</sup>C tracing and predicted CUE from 126 metagenome-assembled genomes using flux balance analysis, alongside 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing. Despite differences in soil properties and microbial community composition between sites, neither predicted nor measured CUE differed significantly between the low- and high-MAP soils. Urine addition increased measured CUE from 0.13 to 0.30 by day 72 in the low-MAP soil, whereas the corresponding change in the high-MAP soil was not significant relative to control samples. Urine also altered community composition, especially in the low-MAP soil where phylum Firmicutes temporarily increased in relative abundance by up to 70 %. Overall, median predicted CUE (0.15) was similar to the measured CUE (0.19), but the genome-based predictions did not capture the time- or urine-associated increases. These findings suggest that genome-resolved models can provide a useful baseline for estimating microbial CUE but may not predict changes in actual C allocation following shifts in resource availability. Therefore, predicting soil C dynamics under changing precipitation regimes requires considering both microbial community composition and context-dependent responses to resource availability.

The repository contains input files and pipeline for calculating <sup>13</sup>C-HWEC estimated CUE and predicted metagenome-based CUE.
